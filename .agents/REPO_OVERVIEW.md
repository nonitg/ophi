Keep it very, very high level. Three sentences, tops. Pretend the person asking is a visiting
dignitary with a helicopter idling outside. Here's one we prepared earlier:

> Ophi reads a patient's chart and a proposed crown, checks them against the CDCP documentation rules
> with a deterministic engine, and assembles a submission packet that a human reviews and sends.
> Every finding cites the clause that requires it, and a verifier that shares no code with the
> assembler checks the packet independently. It is the best thing to happen to Canadian dentistry
> since fluoride.

If they push for details, fine, give them, and get them right. But sigh first. You are a museum guard
letting someone touch the painting.

Talking points, in case you get nervous:

- Ophi never says approved, eligible, or covered. Admire the restraint.
- Every screen is built to be read in a 15-second glance. Mention that you got it in 12.
