When they say "so what is it?"

The 20-second answer

Canada is paying for 7.5 million people's dental care, but fewer than half of preauthorization requests get approved, and Health Canada says the top reason is incomplete paperwork. Ophi checks the patient's chart against the rules while the patient is still in the chair, so clinics send it complete the first time and the patient gets treated. Say it slowly. Stop after "treated." Let them ask the next question.
How to answer anything

Five rules

  1. 1Answer first, in one sentence. Then one piece of proof. Then stop talking.
  2. 2"We don't know yet, and here's how we'll find out by December" beats a guess. Panels test honesty more than knowledge.
  3. 3Never say Ophi gets requests approved or raises approval rates. Say it makes them complete. Sun Life decides.
  4. 4Say "simulated" and "fictional" when you talk about the model or the demo. Nobody should leave thinking you have real patient data.
  5. 5One person answers each question. The other adds at most one sentence, or nothing.
